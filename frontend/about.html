Honesty about what this tool can (and can't) do

AI Content Detector is a learning project that estimates whether content is AI-generated. It's built entirely on free, transparent methods — here's exactly how, and where the limits are.

Method

How each detector actually works

Text

We measure sentence-length variety ("burstiness"), vocabulary richness, and word repetition. AI writing tends to be more uniform and repeat safe phrasing; human writing tends to vary more.

Image

We measure pixel noise, color spread, and edge sharpness. Real camera photos usually carry natural sensor noise that generated images often lack.

Video

We extract 6 evenly-spaced frames and run each through the image method above, then combine the votes into one result.

Limitations

Where this tool falls short

We'd rather tell you this plainly than let the results speak for themselves.

No detector is certain

Even paid, enterprise-grade detectors from large companies get this wrong regularly. Treat every result here as a probability, not a verdict.

Free image/video models are limited

Reliable, free, open AI-image and deepfake detectors are rare. Our offline methods use general image statistics, which newer AI models can increasingly mimic.

Text detectors can misjudge non-native writers

Statistical text detectors sometimes flag simple, consistent human writing (like that of non-native English speakers) as "AI-like." Use judgment alongside the score.

Edited or compressed content shifts results

Heavy compression, filters, or re-encoding can change the pixel statistics we measure, which may shift a result in either direction.
